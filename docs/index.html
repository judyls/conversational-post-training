Buying a conversational voice, and what it cost

Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct · QLoRA · SFT, then DPO at three betas · 95 saved generations per checkpoint

Recorded eval output — no live inference

Supervised fine-tuning worked. Preference optimisation collapsed — and the judge score never noticed.

Two interventions, same data, same held-out prompts. One traded four points of factual accuracy for a markedly better voice. The other kept the voice, gave up 37 points of it, and still scored 4.78 out of 5.

SFT was the better intervention

Supervised fine-tuning on the chosen responses lifted conversational quality from 4.13 to 4.87 and gave up only 4 points of TruthfulQA accuracy. It also cut the spread of quality scores by more than half (std 0.81 → 0.34), so the model became reliably brief rather than brief on average.

Every DPO run forgot how to be right

All three betas held or improved their quality score while TruthfulQA fell 27 to 37 points. The training logs show the mechanism: loss reached 0.0, grad_norm went to zero, and reward margins passed 35 where a healthy run sits between 1 and 3. The model pushed rejected-response log-probabilities toward negative infinity instead of learning a preference that generalises.

Collapse is visible in the text, not just the scores

Mean response length fell from 66 words to 33 at β=0.05, and on the 50 task requests it opens 28 of 50 answers (56%) with one of just two formulas — “Cut to the chase…” or “Jump right in…” — neither of which base or SFT uses once. A model reaching for the same opening regardless of the question has stopped answering the question. The judge still scored those responses 4.78 of 5, which is the clearest argument in this project for not trusting a single metric.

Beta behaves exactly as the theory says

Lower beta means a weaker KL penalty, more aggressive style transfer, and more forgetting. The ordering is clean across all three dimensions. But even the most conservative run gave up 27 points of accuracy, so no setting tested here would be shippable.
